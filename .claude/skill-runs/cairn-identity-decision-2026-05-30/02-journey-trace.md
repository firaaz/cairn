## Ranked mechanism gaps (most load-bearing first)

### Gap 1 — The lead-anchoring re-injection hook does not exist (the cycle's entire reason-for-being is unmechanized). **FATAL-AS-DESIGNED / fixable-in-ADR.**
§6 names a turn-level hook re-injecting `goal:`+`amendments:` as "the one lever that actually holds" and "mechanical (reliable)." **No such hook is wired** (`settings.json` has only PreToolUse/PostToolUse). The cycle's headline claim — "goal-commitment against reactivity" — rests on a mechanism that is currently a *prompt-discipline aspiration*. Concrete failure: after auto-compaction, the committed goal fades from the lead's window; the amendment protocol (also discipline-only) runs against a degraded goal; whipsaw returns. The fix is small and known (a `UserPromptSubmit`/`SessionStart` hook emitting `additionalContext` from the charter file — gh:#33 D1's unshipped SessionStart skill is the same machinery), so this is **fixable in the ADR**, but the ADR MUST commit the hook as a build deliverable, not narrate it as already-mechanical. As written, it is a fatal gap between claim and substrate.

### Gap 6 — In-cycle /decision route re-introduces the rejected upstream-asymmetry collapse. **FATAL (design contradiction) unless re-scoped.**
`phase-lock-and-role-declaration` rejected a "Pre-Decision phase" precisely because decisions are UPSTREAM of slices. §7's "spin a /decision sub-arc between charter-commit and build, it's one cycle because `done:` spans the arc" nests a full decision pipeline inside a work-cycle — the same collapse, relabeled. Concrete failure: a decision and its implementation share one `done:` and one lead context, so the decision's adversarial independence (cairn's C7 core) is pulled into the same window that will build against it — author≠critic erosion at the arc level. This is not a missing mechanism; it's a **contradiction with a standing ADR**, and the identity ADR cannot adopt the charter cycle without either (a) routing decision-goals as a *separate upstream cycle* that the build-cycle then consumes (preserving the asymmetry), or (b) explicitly superseding `phase-lock-and-role-declaration`'s upstream principle with a defense. Left as-is, it's fatal.

### Gap 3 — Return-channel accumulation on the single lead defeats isolation across the pipeline (C3 multi-phase residual). **ACCEPT-AND-DOCUMENT residual, hardenable.**
Arc A's C3 verdict ("subagents can't re-pollute the parent") holds **per single dispatch** but explicitly degrades when one lead accumulates each phase's return across a pipeline — and the charter cycle has the lead orchestrating harvest→(decision)→build→verify. Each return is capped, but caps *sum*: by cycle-close the lead's persistent window holds formation chat + every worker's return. The four provisos meant to bound this — (a) committed-artifact channel, (b) capped returns, (c) foreground+named, (d) fork-hostile — are **disciplined prose in the current skill, enforced by no hook**, and proviso (a)/(b) live in a SKILL.md that the charter cycle proposes to *rewrite*. Concrete failure: accumulated return-context (worst case: an in-window /decision sub-arc, Gap-6/B8) re-pollutes the lead, and the lead is also the goal-anchor (Gap 1) and the build≠check gate (A8) — three roles on one degrading context. The honest disposition is **accept-and-document** (it is a real residual cairn shares with all single-orchestrator designs) PLUS harden by making proviso (a) mechanical: a hook/contract that rejects worker returns exceeding the cap and requires a commit_hash, so the artifact-channel is enforced not assumed.

### Gap 2 — No per-worker write-scope; `AGENT_ROLE` is dead, blind workers are gated only by the worktree-global operator envelope. **FIXABLE-IN-ADR (this is open-question #2).**
A "blind worker" sounds sandboxed but isn't: its writes are checked against `active-envelope.yaml` (worktree-global), not a per-worker path set. `AGENT_ROLE` is read by `role_guard.py:137` but **injected nowhere** — confirmed dead. Agent `tools:` frontmatter is a *capability* allowlist (can it Write at all), not a *path* allowlist (where). Concrete failure: a build worker scoped in its brief to `operational-reference.md` can write anywhere the envelope permits (all `docs/`, `checks/`, `tests/`, `.claude/`); the only thing stopping scope-creep writes is the worker's own obedience. The §9 open question (native `tools`/`permissionMode`/`disallowedTools` vs fix the `SubagentStart`/env wiring) must be **decided in the ADR**, not deferred — otherwise the "blind worker" gains no write-confinement over a normal session. Fixable, but load-bearing because the design sells worker-isolation as a safety property it doesn't currently have on the write axis.

## Lower-severity gaps (documented, not ranked into the top tier)

- **Gap 4 — `done:` singularity leaks through operator rubber-stamp.** "One testable `done:` = one goal" is declared-not-derived; fuzzy cases ("ship X + document X") go to the operator. If the operator rubber-stamps a two-goal charter, two goals share one cycle and the amendment protocol legitimizes mid-cycle whipsaw between them — the exact failure the cycle exists to prevent, now *inside the lines*. No mechanism; intrinsically discipline-only (semantic). **Accept-and-document**; the leverage (force the judgment once at commit) is real but the floor is operator rigor.
- **Gap 5 — Charter ↔ pointer-handoff sync burden.** Two state-carrying artifacts per in-flight cycle (charter = within-cycle goal-state; handoff = cross-cycle pointers). The split is conceptually clean (§9 asserts it), but consistency at close is disciplined, not enforced; an abandoned mid-cycle charter with a stale handoff pointer is possible. Low severity; **accept-and-document**, with a note that the handoff contract test (`test_handoff_contract.py`) does not know charters exist.
- **B4/B7 — route-misclassify and non-atomic ADR+invariant.** A goal misrouted as within-constraints skips the ADR; "same commit" for ADR+invariant is convention. Both caught *eventually* by the validator only if a new invariant assertion is reached. Fixable by making the route a checklist gate and the atomicity a validator pre-condition.

---

## One-line verdict for synthesis

The charter cycle's two **mechanical** anchors (append-only ADR guard, validator gate) are real and survive. Its three **headline** safety claims — lead-anchoring (Gap 1), worker write-isolation (Gap 2), and cross-pipeline isolation (Gap 3) — are **unbuilt, dead, and assumed** respectively; and its substrate-coupling route **contradicts a standing ADR** (Gap 6). None is unfixable, but the ADR cannot *narrate* these as mechanical — it must either commit them as build deliverables (Gaps 1, 2), enforce the proviso (Gap 3), or re-scope the route (Gap 6).
