## 1 — The case: why A is the right identity + methodology for cairn now

### 1.1 The honest reframe is the whole argument — and it is *stronger*, not weaker, than "phases-out"

The lead's F2 (`00-lead-findings.md:8-10`) is not a counter-argument against A. It is **A's own thesis, stated precisely**. brainstorm-1's TL;DR already says "phases-out" oversells; the lead's "machinery-out, cleaves-preserved-via-subagents" is the correct claim and it is what A advocates. Drop the strawman and the mapping is exact:

| 4-phase cognitive mode (phase-pipeline-evaluation says "resists compression") | Charter-cycle carrier | Preserved? |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** (Reader) | charter-formation brainstorming → committed `goal:`/`done:` (`brainstorm-1:§4`) | YES — *co-formed with operator*, then frozen at commit |

**This is the move that defeats the phase-pipeline-evaluation counter-evidence.** That ADR (`01-constraint-envelope.md:16` / phase-pipeline-evaluation:100,104) found the four *cognitive modes* are valuable and "resist compression" on 5 slices. A **agrees and preserves all four.** What phase-pipeline-evaluation never tested — because in 2026-04 the primitive to test it did not exist — is whether those four modes must be carried by **session boundaries + `AGENT_ROLE` + per-phase commit gates + a fixed SKILL.md**, or whether they can be carried by **subagent dispatches gated by a charter**. The ADR is the heaviest counter-evidence against *dropping cognitive modes*; it is silent on the *delivery substrate*, and silent because it predates arc A's native-subagent landscape. A keeps every finding phase-pipeline-evaluation actually established and changes only the thing it never examined. The lead states this exact rebuttal at `00-lead-findings.md:10` ("it confirms the cognitive modes are valuable, NOT that they must be SESSION boundaries vs subagent dispatches, and it predates arc A's native-subagent landscape").

### 1.2 The one pivotal fact that makes A buildable now and was not buildable in 2026-04

C3-as-written — "subagents are the WRONG tool for linear pipeline boundaries because their return summaries pollute the parent" — is **`now-redundant`, and it is the *only* one of cairn's eight load-bearing claims the skeptic-verifier tried to refute and could not** (`landscape.md:87,98`: "the only claim the verifier *tried to refute and could not*, returning `verified: true`"). Every other "redundant"-leaning verdict was either downgraded by the verifier (C2) or never skeptic-verified at all (C5–C8, `verified: null`). C3 is the single most-rigorously-established verdict in the entire landscape, and it is the one that licenses A.

The mechanism is structural, not a model improvement that could regress: each subagent runs in a fresh window and **only the final message returns** — the reasoning trace C3 feared cannot physically re-enter the parent (`landscape.md:98`). Cairn's *own* spec walks the v1 claim back (spec-v1 §53 → spec-v2 §63 "v1 made this claim and v2 retracts it"). The original, foundational objection to a subagent-based methodology is gone, and it is gone on the strongest evidence in the corpus. **A is the approach that takes the one verified-true finding and builds on it.** A comparator that keeps session-boundary machinery is paying full ceremony cost to defend against a hazard the most-tested verdict in arc A says no longer exists.

### 1.3 The methodology gains a value the 4 phases only defended *by accident*

The 4 phases defend goal-stability **incidentally** — via isolation, as a side-effect of resetting context at each boundary. They never named input-reactive over-steering as a target (`brainstorm-1:§1`). The charter cycle names it and mechanizes it: the goal is committed once, defended by surface-don't-switch, and **re-injected every turn so it survives compaction** (`brainstorm-1:§5-§6`). This is not a rebrand — it is a behavioral mechanism with standalone value (the contract: the lead *names* a proposed pivot — "committed goal is X; this reads as a shift to Y — amend or fold?" — rather than silently complying or silently ignoring). Even a skeptic who rejects the identity *headline* must concede the amendment protocol is a real, buildable control mechanism that the superseded machinery did not provide. This is the answer to R2's "framing-only" charge (see §3.S1, §4): A ships behavior, not just a label.

### 1.4 The "complement" positioning is the correct, honest identity claim — and it is *defensible* precisely because it is additive

A does **not** claim goal-commitment replaces the cliff thesis. It positions the two as co-equal (`brainstorm-1:TL;DR, §10-Q7`). This is the conservative, evidence-respecting position: arc A confirms the cliff (C1) and the cross-slice substrate (C5–C8) remain load-bearing, and A leaves all substrate internals untouched (`brainstorm-1:§9` — ADR corpus, append-only guard, validator, `/decision`, pointer-handoff all survive). Cairn's durable identity (the cross-slice coherence contract) is *preserved verbatim*; A adds a second, within-cycle value that the runtime has now made cheap to mechanize. The pre-mortem's deepest finding (`03-premortem.md:59`) — that the headline and the load-bearing identity point at different failure-mode axes — is real and I engage it head-on at S2 below; but note it argues for *keeping the cliff thesis load-bearing*, which is **exactly what A's complement framing does.** A does not bet the farm on the soft axis; it adds the soft axis beside the hard one.

### S1 — Headline value unmeasurable; one-way door defended by prose. **[high×high] — NARROWS (was SURVIVES-as-written)**

The pre-mortem's headline (`03:27-29`): "goal-commitment vs reactivity" is a counterfactual about one operator's sessions with no baseline; after superseding 3 firm ADRs the door is irreversible and the claim rests on prose.

**What A's design changes:**
1. **Reframe the identity claim to dissolve the unmeasurability premise.** The honest claim (§1.1) is not "we reduced reactivity by X%" — it is "the lead now *surfaces* proposed pivots and *records* amendments instead of silently complying." That is **directly observable and falsifiable per-turn**, not a counterfactual: open the `amendments:` log; either pivots were surfaced-and-recorded or they were silently folded. The mechanism's *operation* is measurable even if its *behavioral lift* is not. S1 attacks the lift; A's defensible claim is the operation.
2. **A falsifiable instrument I commit A to ship (mitigation):** an `amendments:`-log audit at cycle-close — count (a) amendments recorded with a reason vs (b) goal-changes detectable in the commit history that were *not* recorded as amendments. A rising (b) is the canary that the protocol is decaying to ad-hoc (this is exactly S5's "first amendment recorded as '(see chat)'" signal, `03:45`, turned into a standing instrument). This is a cheap, structural (not semantic) check — expressible as a `git-log-walk` + `structural-parser` assertion *inside the closed whitelist* (F6), so it is buildable without a new validator-type ADR.
3. **The one-way-door cost is real but mis-attributed by S1.** S1 says the door swings on "an unmeasurable hinge." But the *irreversible* part — retiring INV-003, superseding phase-lock — is justified by §1.2's verified-true C3 finding, **not** by the soft headline. If the headline proves worthless, the *methodology* (subagent-carried cognitive modes, build≠check, validator gate) still stands on C3+C2, and the ADR can be *amended at frontmatter* to demote the identity claim from co-equal to scoped-sub-claim without unwinding the supersessions. The complement framing (§1.4) is what makes this graceful: the cliff thesis is preserved, so demoting the second value does not collapse the identity.

**Honest residual:** the *behavioral-lift* claim ("less whippy") remains unmeasurable in a single-operator repo. A narrows S1 from "fatal one-way door on an unmeasurable hinge" to "an unmeasurable secondary claim riding on a measurable mechanism and a verified-true methodology change." It does **not** fully close it. **Verdict: NARROWS.**

The mechanisms that *do* fight cross-cycle drift — the append-only ADR corpus, the derived ARCHITECTURE.md, the invariant validator (C6) — **all survive A intact.** A adds a within-cycle value *beside* the cross-cycle one; it does not substitute one for the other. ### S3 — `done:`-singularity + same-model verify rubber-stamps; build≠check is role-purity, not decorrelation. **[high×med-high] — NARROWS, with a concrete mitigation A should commit**

The pre-mortem (`03:35-37`) and the cross-cutting note (`03:61`) land the sharpest technical blow: build≠check buys **role-PURITY, not error-DECORRELATION.** A same-model fresh-context reviewer shares ~60% weights-level blind spots with the builder (Kim et al., ICML 2025; `landscape.md:78,108`). L-016 ("Verdict-OK-but-bugs-present") *already fired* on the downstream consumer. So A's mandatory cleave is, as the pre-mortem says, "its weakest correlated-error defense," and A "canonizes it as the mandatory invariant."

**I concede the core: build≠check ≠ decorrelation.** This is true and I will not hedge it. But three things narrow the blow:

1. **A is not *less* decorrelated than what it supersedes on the axis that matters — and on one axis it can be *more*.** The pre-mortem says A "removes the 4-phase Auditor *and* the human role-reset half and replaces them with one same-model blind verifier — strictly less decorrelation" (`03:37`). But the human role-reset half is **unfalsifiable** (spec-v1 §17; `landscape.md:133`) — it was never a *measurable* decorrelation gain to lose. And the 4-phase Auditor was *also* same-model (the whole pipeline ran one model family). So the superseded machinery had **the same ~60% correlated-error floor** A does. A does not regress decorrelation; it inherits the identical floor on a lighter substrate.
2. **The concrete mitigation A should commit — cross-MODEL verify.** The landscape names this exactly: "Cross-MODEL review is an under-exploited extension… neither cairn nor any native runtime ENFORCES cross-model review" (`landscape.md:134`; residual leg #1, `landscape.md:108`: "The strongest form of the bet needs cross-MODEL review"). The charter cycle's blind-worker architecture makes this **trivially mechanizable in a way the session-boundary machinery did not**: per-subagent model selection is a native primitive (`landscape.md:86,134`), so A's ADR can make the **verify-worker run a different model family than the build-worker** — turning build≠check from role-purity into genuine error-decorrelation. This is a single line in the agent-def frontmatter (model tier), not a new mechanism. **A is the *only* approach in this arc that structurally enables enforced cross-model verify**, because it is the only one that replaces fixed session boundaries with per-subagent-configurable dispatches. The comparator (session-bound phases) cannot enforce cross-model review without first becoming subagent-based — i.e., without first becoming A.
3. **The skeptic-worker (test-first, default-on) adds a decorrelation layer the pre-mortem under-weights.** `done:` is turned into an *executable* acceptance test by a *third* worker before build (`brainstorm-1:§8`). A failing-then-passing executable test is not a same-model judgment call — it is a deterministic gate. The shared-blind-spot risk applies to *semantic* "did it meet `done:`" judgments; it does not apply to "does the executable test the skeptic wrote pass." S3's L-016 shape (semantic verdict-OK-but-bugs) is real for the *interpretive* part of verify, but the test-first layer converts much of `done:` into a mechanical check that correlated weights cannot rubber-stamp.

**Honest residual:** for the *interpretive* slice of verification (does the commit *truly* satisfy `done:`'s intent, beyond the executable test), same-model correlation persists unless cross-model verify is actually shipped — and shipping it is a build deliverable A must commit, not narrate. **Verdict: NARROWS — and A is uniquely positioned to *close* it via cross-model verify, which no comparator can do without adopting A's subagent substrate first.**
